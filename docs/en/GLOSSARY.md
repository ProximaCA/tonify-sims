# Glossary

## rightsholder

Whoever legally owns the recording and receives the platform's payment. Usually a label, sometimes the artist. Every payout figure in this study is a payment to the *rightsholder*; what reaches the artist depends on the contract.

## pro-rata

The dominant payout rule: all subscription money goes into one pool, and each rightsholder receives the pool multiplied by their share of total platform plays. A listener's own money is not tied to what that listener played.

## user-centric

The proposed alternative: each subscriber's wallet is divided only among the artists that subscriber actually played. The same total pool, distributed by a different rule — user-centric is a pure redistribution, never a source of new money.

## artist-centric

A family of variants that add conditions on top of a pool rule — minimum stream thresholds, multipliers for artists above a size, exclusions for non-music audio. Deezer's version is the reference implementation.

## label pass-through

The fraction of a rightsholder payment that actually reaches the artist under their contract. In this study it is a multiplier applied after the payout rule; the study's second lemma is about what reforms can and cannot do to it.

## 360 deal

A contract in which the label takes a share not only of recording revenue but of touring, merchandise and other artist income. It widens the gap between rightsholder payment and artist income beyond the recording pass-through.

## superfan

A listener who pays an artist directly — a donation, a paid subscription, a tip — rather than only streaming them. In the direct-economy model, each audience member becomes a superfan independently with a fixed probability.

## royalty

The payment owed for use of a recording. In streaming it is computed by the payout rule from plays, not from the price any individual listener paid.

## pool

The sum of net subscriber wallets available for distribution in a period. Both pro-rata and user-centric divide the *same* pool; the direct mechanism sits outside it entirely.

## MVA (minimum viable audience)

The audience size an artist needs for their income to cross a chosen annual threshold. It is the quantity where the contract shows up most brutally: the same threshold demands a very different audience for a signed artist than for an independent one.
