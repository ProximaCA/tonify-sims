\section{Problem statement}
The primary task is to generate a listening matrix satisfying specified
hard constraints and statistical targets. On each accepted matrix we
compare two deterministic rules allocating the same guaranteed budget:
pro-rata (PR), which allocates the pooled budget in proportion to total
artist plays, and user-centric (UC), which allocates each listener's wallet
in proportion to that listener's plays.

PR and UC are alternative allocations of the same budget. Direct artist
payments can coexist with either rule and are outside this initial study.
Wallets are net amounts available for allocation after any funding fee;
subscription tiers, service fees and label contracts are deferred.
The full previous treatment is retained in \texttt{THEORY.v0.5.md}.
No novelty claim is made here for the elementary identities or inequalities.

\section{Conceptual formulation}
A matrix cell measures either play counts or total listening duration for
one artist--listener pair during one common observation period. Units
must be consistent across the matrix. A generator may produce a random
matrix; once realized, payouts and weighted means are finite deterministic
calculations. Behavioral responses to changing the payment rule are not modeled.

Marginal activity distributions do not determine which listeners are
connected to which artists. A useful generator must also expose targets
for degrees, nonzero pairs, concentration and dependence. Matching a few
statistics is not evidence that a matrix represents the actual music market.

\subsection{Definitions and edge cases}
Let $N$ be the number of artists, $U$ the number of listeners, and
$p_{iu}\ge0$ the activity of listener $u$ on artist $i$ in a common period.
Rows are artists; columns are listeners. Legacy sim4 sparse exports use
the opposite orientation and must be transposed.
Define listener totals $P_u=\sum_i p_{iu}>0$, artist totals
$P_i=\sum_u p_{iu}$, total activity $T=\sum_uP_u=\sum_iP_i$, and mean
listener activity $\Pbar=T/U$. For a common positive net wallet $W$ and
budget $R=UW$,
\[
PR_i=UW\frac{P_i}{T},\qquad UC_i=W\sum_u\frac{p_{iu}}{P_u}.
\]
Here $PR_i$ and $UC_i$ are artist $i$'s payouts. These are deterministic
functions of the matrix and wallet. If $P_i=0$, both payouts vanish but
their ratio is undefined. With audience size $A_i=\#\{u:p_{iu}>0\}$,
$UC_i\le WA_i$ since every share $p_{iu}/P_u\le1$.

\textbf{Budget balance follows from the definitions:}
\[
\sum_i PR_i=UW,\qquad
\sum_i UC_i=W\sum_u\frac{\sum_i p_{iu}}{P_u}=UW.
\]
Thus $\sum_i(UC_i-PR_i)=0$; this is not a corollary of Theorem 1.
For individual net wallets $W_u\ge0$ with $R=\sum_uW_u>0$, define
$PR_i=RP_i/T$ and $UC_i=\sum_u W_up_{iu}/P_u$. The same budget identity holds.

\subsection{Scope and assumptions}
\textbf{A1: active listeners and consistent units.} Every included listener
has $P_u>0$. The integer prototype uses $p_{iu}\in\mathbb Z_+$, hence
$P_u\ge1$, combining former A1 and A3. The algebra also permits real
listening durations. Activity thresholds, silent payers (``deaf listeners'')
and routing their money to the service are deferred.

\textbf{A2: common net wallets in the main comparison.} Theorem 1 uses a
common $W$. Individual wallets use the extension above. Declining service
fees and superfan subscription tiers are deferred.

Former A4 and A7 are incorporated into the definition of a deterministic
payout on one matrix. Former A5 concerns direct payments and is deferred.
Service fees are already deducted; former A6 was a separate label-contract
assumption, also outside this experiment. Former A8 does not prescribe a
universal tail law: distribution families and their parameters are generator
inputs. Condition (C) belongs only to the deferred tail lemma L3 in v0.5,
not to the conceptual hypotheses of this experiment.

\subsection{Finite arithmetic on one matrix}
\paragraph{L1: payout ratio and UC gain conditions.}
\why{For an accepted matrix, payout changes can be computed exactly. Each
listener's weight is their share of a particular artist's plays. An
unweighted average over people cannot replace this calculation.}

\begin{thmone}
Under A1--A2, for artist $i$ with $P_i>0$, let
$w^i_u=p_{iu}/P_i$ be normalized nonnegative weights,
$m_i=\sum_u w^i_uP_u$ the weighted arithmetic mean, and
$H_i=(\sum_u w^i_u/P_u)^{-1}$ the weighted harmonic mean. Then
\[
\frac{UC_i}{PR_i}
=\Pbar\sum_u\frac{w^i_u}{P_u}
=\frac{\Pbar}{H_i}
=\frac{\Pbar}{m_i}\frac{m_i}{H_i}.
\]
\end{thmone}
\begin{proof}
Dividing $W\sum_u p_{iu}/P_u$ by $UWP_i/T$ and canceling common factors\newline
gives $(T/U)\sum_u(p_{iu}/P_i)/P_u$. Substitute the definitions.
All operations are finite sums; no stochastic interpretation is required.
\end{proof}

\textbf{A sufficient bound.} Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
$\sqrt{w^i_uP_u}$ and $\sqrt{w^i_u/P_u}$ gives
\[
\left(\sum_u w^i_uP_u\right)\left(\sum_u\frac{w^i_u}{P_u}\right)
\ge\left(\sum_u w^i_u\right)^2=1.
\]
Hence $H_i\le m_i$ and $UC_i/PR_i\ge\Pbar/m_i$. Equality of the means
holds exactly when $P_u$ is constant on the positive-weight support.
Therefore $m_i\le\Pbar$ is sufficient for $UC_i\ge PR_i$;
$m_i<\Pbar$ guarantees a strict gain. An \emph{upper} bound on $m_i$ helps
verify this condition; for example $\max_{u:p_{iu}>0}P_u\le\Pbar$ suffices.
A lower bound alone does not establish it.

\textbf{(a) Equality.} For a given artist, $UC_i=PR_i$ exactly when
$\sum_u p_{iu}(1/P_u-U/T)=0$. Simultaneous equality is $pc=0$, with
$c_u=1/P_u-U/T$: $c$ belongs to the kernel of $p$. Constant listener
activity is sufficient but not necessary: $p=(10,20)$ with one artist
gives $UC_1=PR_1=2W$.

\textbf{(c) Exact crossover.} $UC_i\ge PR_i$ if and only if $H_i\le\Pbar$;
strict inequality corresponds to $H_i<\Pbar$. The condition involving
$m_i$ is only sufficient. Labels (a) and (c) are retained for comparison
with the review; former (b) is now part of the definitions.

\textbf{(d) Interpretation.} If $m_i=\Pbar$, then $UC_i/PR_i=m_i/H_i\ge1$,
strictly so when listener totals differ on the positive-weight support.
This describes a particular weighted sequence. Changing a matrix changes
its weights, means and total activity simultaneously. It does not imply
that increasing dispersion always increases UC income. The former
``dispersion premium'' claim is withdrawn.

\textbf{The review's coefficient-of-variation bound.} For the standard
finite definition $CV_i^2=\sum_u w^i_u(P_u-m_i)^2/m_i^2$, the proposed
$m_i/H_i\ge1+CV_i^2$ is false. Take
\[
p=\begin{pmatrix}9&1\\1&99\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Artist 1 has $P_u=(10,100)$, $w^1=(0.9,0.1)$, $m_1=19$ and
$H_1=1000/91$. Thus $m_1/H_1=1.729$, while
$1+CV_1^2=1090/361\approx3.01939$. The review omitted its CV definition;
this counterexample applies to the definition explicitly stated here.
Expanding the square instead yields the correct identity
\[
\frac{m_i}{H_i}-1
=\sum_u w^i_u\frac{(P_u-m_i)^2}{m_iP_u}\ge0.
\]
The denominators differ from those of $CV_i^2$.
With unequal wallets, the general ratio is
$UC_i/PR_i=(T/R)\sum_u w^i_uW_u/P_u$; the unadjusted condition
$H_i\le\Pbar$ no longer characterizes its sign.

\whatitsays{The matrix, or its artist-specific weighted audience means,
is needed to determine the winner. Niche status, audience size or
heterogeneity alone is insufficient. Both payouts and their common budget
are checked after accepting a matrix; the desired direction of payout
redistribution is not an optimization target.}

\section{Constrained matrix generation}
\subsection{Inputs and targets}
The standalone \texttt{sim4/constrained\_gen.py} takes artist and listener
counts, total integer activity, a common period, seed, iteration budget,
search parameters and statistical targets. Optionally all listener totals
are fixed. Every move preserves nonnegative integer cells, total activity
and positive listener totals; fixed column totals are also preserved.
Impossible hard budgets are rejected before search. Real durations are
supported by the payout formulas, not by this integer optimizer.

Distribution targets specify a family, parameters, fixed comparison grid,
tolerance, positive weight and provenance (measured, assumed or derived).
Scalar targets specify a value instead of a CDF. Supported families are
normal and lognormal (\texttt{mu}, \texttt{sigma}; log-scale for lognormal),
Pareto (\texttt{shape}, \texttt{scale}), and beta (\texttt{alpha}, \texttt{beta}).
Optional truncation precedes optional upward integer rounding.

Available distributions cover listener and artist totals, listener degrees,
artist audiences, positive pair counts, positive artist-specific weights,
artist weight concentration, listener preference concentration, and
weighted arithmetic and harmonic audience activity. Scalar metrics are
zero fraction, top-artist play share, artist-total Gini and the correlation
of log audience size with log mean plays per audience member. Undefined
correlations fail acceptance. Artist means and weight concentrations
exclude zero-play artists; artist totals and audience sizes include them.
Pooled positive weights do not prescribe a separate law for every artist.
The full metric contract is in \texttt{sim4/CONSTRAINED.md}.

\subsection{Objective and stochastic search}
For target $j$, let $Z_j(p)$ be its finite sequence of values,
$F_j$ its specified CDF, $G_j$ a fixed threshold grid and
$\widehat F_j(x;p)=\#\{z\in Z_j(p):z\le x\}/|Z_j(p)|$ its empirical CDF.
Define
\[
r_j(p)=\max_{x\in G_j}|\widehat F_j(x;p)-F_j(x)|,
\qquad
J(p)=\frac{\sum_j\lambda_j[r_j(p)/\varepsilon_j]^2}{\sum_j\lambda_j},
\]
where $\varepsilon_j>0$ is the tolerance and $\lambda_j>0$ its weight.
For a scalar target, $r_j$ is its absolute error. Acceptance requires
\emph{every} residual to satisfy its tolerance, as well as every hard
constraint. A small aggregate objective alone is insufficient.

The CDF error is a maximum over a declared grid, not over the entire real
line. It is not a hypothesis test or a p-value: cells are dependent and
the matrix is fitted to the same targets. Standard KS test interpretation
is inapplicable (see \href{https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.kstest.html}{SciPy's KS documentation}).

Starting from a random feasible integer matrix, transfer a positive integer
amount between two cells. A donor cannot become negative or leave its
listener inactive. With fixed listener totals, transfers stay in the same
column. Improvements are accepted; worsening moves are accepted with
probability $\exp(-\Delta J/\theta_t)$, where $\theta_t>0$ decreases over
the iteration budget. This is a stochastic optimization heuristic with an
annealing acceptance rule, not a sampler with a claimed stationary law.

The first candidate satisfying all tolerances takes priority over a
lower-loss candidate failing any tolerance. Otherwise the lowest-loss
candidate is returned with \texttt{accepted: false}. The trace records the
lowest encountered objective and the selected candidate's objective
separately. Failure does not prove infeasibility; success does not prove
global optimality.

In classical \href{https://staff.washington.edu/paymana/swarm/kennedy95-ijcnn.pdf}{Kennedy--Eberhart PSO}, a particle represents a complete solution and its
coordinates are decision variables. A direct PSO implementation would
therefore use a whole matrix per particle. ``One cell per particle'' needs
a separately specified cooperative scheme. We implement the review's
allowed alternative stochastic algorithm; no PSO comparison is claimed.

\subsection{Calibration and limitations}
Targets must use matching periods, units and aggregation levels.
The sum of artist totals equals the sum of listener totals; the sum of
artist audiences equals the sum of listener degrees. For integer counts,
$A_i\le\min(U,P_i)$ and $K_u\le\min(N,P_u)$.
Thus independently proposed marginal laws can conflict.

In \href{https://www.cp.jku.at/people/schedl/Research/Publications/pdf/schedl_icmr_2016.pdf}{Schedl (2016), LFM-1b, Table 6}, 21.21 and 5.16 describe the mean and median
of \emph{user-level average plays per artist}. They are not the mean and
median of the pooled positive cell counts, do not establish lognormality,
and are not automatically annual statistics. The legacy cell-law fit
based on these numbers is not used as an empirical target here. Likewise,
track-level Luminate shares cannot be transferred to artists without an
aggregation model. Existing emp1 slopes are not interchangeable with the
correlation metric used here.

The included example is entirely assumed. Dense storage and full statistic
recomputation support small methodological experiments only. Per-artist
conditional CDFs, higher-order dependencies and time structure are not
specified. Marginal acceptance does not identify a joint distribution or
produce uniform samples from the feasible set.

\subsection{Verification and reproduction}
From the repository root:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 -m unittest sim4.test_constrained_gen -v
python3 sim4/constrained_gen.py \
  --config sim4/constrained_example.json --output work/constrained-demo
\end{verbatim}
The outputs are \texttt{matrix.csv} (artist by listener),
\texttt{payouts.csv} (per-artist PR, UC, ratio and weighted means at $W=1$),
and \texttt{report.json}: full configuration, seed, library versions, matrix
SHA-256, initial and final residuals, search history and payout gates.
Exit codes are 0 for acceptance, 1 for a saved failed candidate, and 2
for invalid configuration.

Tests cover random finite-matrix identities, budget balance, mean
inequalities and their exact gap, the kernel criterion, the CV
counterexample, zero artists, real durations, unequal wallets,
reproducibility, fixed listener totals, tied counts in CDF comparisons,
and explicit failure on an unattainable target.

The assumed seed-42 example has $N=12$, $U=48$, $T=1200$ and passes all
seven targets after 4080 mutation proposals (1222 accepted). Its objective
falls from 46.661859 to 0.532441 and the payout-ratio identity has maximum
absolute error $4.44\cdot10^{-16}$.
